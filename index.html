How the test worked

The document

Anthropic's S-1 amendment filed with the SEC on 17 November 2026, converted to text: 4,733 lines and about 92,000 words. Read the filing.

RAG and keyword search

Both split the filing into 504 chunks of about 2,000 characters. RAG ranks chunks with the bge-base embedding model, keyword search with BM25. A question counts as found when the answer is inside the top 8 chunks. At 16 chunks RAG finds 8 and keyword search 7.

The agent

Claude Sonnet in Claude Code, with every tool removed except the paper's grep, outline and line reader. Each question ran on its own. The replays show its real tool calls and results at their recorded pace.

Limits

Ten questions on one document, so treat this as a demo, not a benchmark. RAG and keyword search are scored on whether the answer reached the model, the agent on its final answer. The agent is slower and costs more: about 9 seconds and 8 cents a question here, against well under a second for the other two.
